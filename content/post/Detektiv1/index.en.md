Alright folks, grab yourself a mug of tea or hot cocoa, because I've got a story to tell you. Winter in Otaru is straight-up no joke. The weather out there flexes hard. Everything's white, snow as far as the eye can see—total Instagram aesthetic, sure, but also freezing your face off. So cold your nose feels like snapping off the second you step outside. Normally, the temple up on the hillside is the chillest spot ever. Dead quiet, just the occasional chime of a bell and the wind whistling through the old wooden beams. Pure zen, almost spiritual. Today, though? Forget it. Complete chaos mode. Zero inner peace to be found.

# The Crime Scene: Completely Sus and Utterly Impossible

Picture this: a young Hokkaido Prefectural Police officer—let's call him Tanaka—is standing there nearly freezing his ears off. He has zero clue what's going on and looks half a second away from crying. The guy's been on the force for three years; he knows all the usual stuff like bike thieves down by the canal or drunk tourists face-planting into snowbanks. But this? This is next-level weird. Straight-up X-Files material.

In front of this tiny, ancient wooden building, everything's cordoned off with bright yellow police "KEEP OUT" tape. It looks wildly out of place against all that peaceful white, like a glitch in the Matrix. And behind the tape? The snow is smooth as freshly pressed sheets at a five-star hotel. Not a single dent, not a single footprint, nothing. Just a flawless blanket of white. Yet dead center, easily five meters from any solid ground, a massive stone lantern is just... gone.

We're not talking about some cute little IKEA lamp here. This thing is solid granite, easily weighs 50 kilos, and is old enough to have seen samurai walk by. And it just vanished. Poof. Into thin air.

Tanaka's losing his mind because there are literally zero tracks. Zilch. Nada.
No footprints going in, no footprints coming out. Even if a stray cat or a fox had trotted through, you'd see it. The snow is so untouched it's downright creepy. Tanaka's seriously wondering if he's hallucinating or wandered onto the wrong movie set. He checks his notes, checks the snow, checks his notes again. Error 404: Logic not found.

An old monk who looks about a hundred years old is full-on freaking out and mutters in a trembling voice:
> It was a Tengu! A mountain demon swooped down, snatched the lantern, and flew back up into the sky!

Tanaka's first instinct is to say something rational about "physics and all that," except honestly, "demon" is starting to sound more logical than anything else right now. He's half tempted to buy it, lol. I mean, look at the crime scene! How else do you explain it? Teleportation? Aliens?

# Visitors from Tokyo: The Usual Suspects

Suddenly, someone bellows from the back like he owns the whole place:
> A Tengu? Don't talk nonsense! What rubbish! The wind probably just knocked it over and buried it in snow! Or trained crows carried it off!

Enter: a guy you'd recognize instantly if you've turned on a TV even once. That famous private detective from Tokyo—you know the one, with the mustache, always strutting around acting like he's the greatest thing since sliced bread. Let's call him "Master Kogoro" (name changed by the editorial team, *wink*). Naturally, he happens to be on vacation here. Wherever this guy goes, trouble follows. It's like he's a walking disaster magnet. He's wearing a gray suit way too thin for Hokkaido (total rookie mistake—everyone knows you need layers here) and playing the big shot. He swaggers through the snow, nearly eats it, catches himself at the last second, and acts like he meant to do that. Classic.

Tagging along: his daughter (who looks like she could karate-kick somebody straight through a brick wall if they annoyed her) and this little kid with oversized glasses, a blue jacket, and a red bow tie. Let's call him "the kid." The little squirt is somehow built different from normal kids, though. While the adults are arguing and Mustache is yelling at the monk, the kid is scanning the crime scene like a Terminator. Eyes darting back and forth, checking angles, measuring distances in his head. Dude's radiating pure Main Character Energy.

While the "Master" starts rambling about "helicopters with giant magnets" (seriously, bro? Do you even hear yourself?), the kid sneaks quietly around the wooden posts. Pure ninja mode. Nobody pays him any mind because he's tiny. He tugs on the barrier tape to test how taut it is, glances up at the snow hanging off the roof, then crouches down and picks up something off the ground nobody else noticed. Probably some tiny crumb or whatever, yet he stares at it like it's the Holy Grail.

All of a sudden, he smirks down at his wristwatch. Not an "I'm getting ice cream later" smirk—more like a "Game over, I've got you, you are *so* cooked" smirk. Chills. That kid's plotting something, I'd bet money on it.

# It's Showtime: The Sleeping Master Takes the Stage

What happens next? Nobody back at the precinct is ever going to believe Tanaka. If he puts this in his report, they're sending him straight to a psych eval.

Mustache Detective, who was wildly flailing his arms a second ago, suddenly grabs the back of his neck, pulls off a weird ballet-style pirouette (only way clumsier), and—bam—slumps fast asleep against a pillar. Knocked out cold standing up. Mid-sentence. Chin on his chest, eyes shut.

His daughter sighs:
> Dad?! Not again!

And then the guy starts talking. Not in his usual annoying, slurred voice, either—suddenly he sounds razor-sharp, calm, and dead serious. Full-on deep late-night radio voice.

> Yo Tanaka, tell your guys to stop searching the snow. Pointless. Waste of time. Nothing to find, because nobody ever stepped in there.

Completely bewildered, Tanaka instinctively salutes the sleeping man.
> Uh, Master? Then how? Did the thief fly, or what? Was it the Tengu after all?

The voice from off-screen (or from the detective, who knows, his mouth isn't moving at all) replies ice-cold:
> He wasn't even in there. The trick is sitting right in front of your nose, you just can't see it because it's too obvious. Take a good look at the barrier tape. Yeah, that bright yellow stuff right there. Notice anything? Why is that the *only* thing in the entire courtyard without a speck of snow on it, even though it's been snowing like crazy all night?

Tanaka blinks. He looks at the tape. He looks at the posts. He looks at the roof. Mind blown. He's right. Snow is piled on everything—the beams, the eaves, even Tanaka's own cap. Only the yellow tape is clean, smooth, and bone dry.

> The culprit didn't use that tape to cordon off the area,

the sleeping detective continues (though out of the corner of his eye, Tanaka could swear he sees the kid hiding behind a pillar talking into his red bow tie—wtf? Is that a voice modulator?).

> Check out the science behind it: he used the tape as a sled! He looped the wide, heavy-duty tape around the lantern like a lasso while standing at the edge. Then he slid the whole thing across the frozen ice crust underneath the fresh snow right back to himself—without disturbing the snow on top. Smart, right? Physics, bitch! And only *after* that did he wipe the tape clean and string it up, to make it look like an official police line so nobody would notice the snow underneath was churned up. The perfect crime... almost.

# Busted: The Truth Comes Out

Silence. Dead silence in the courtyard. Just the wind whistling softly and a crow cawing somewhere in the distance.
The young monk in charge of courtyard duties goes pale as a sheet. He's shaking, and it's not from the cold. He stares at the yellow tape like it's radioactive. He knows he's done for.

> I... I had gambling debts...,

he stammers quietly, eyes glued to the ground.
> I thought nobody would figure it out in the storm. I thought everyone would blame spirits or call it a miracle. I needed the money...

Tanaka lets out a long breath. The tension evaporates. Case closed. No magic, no demons, no aliens. Just basic physics, a dash of criminal ingenuity, and a guy who's smarter fast asleep than everyone else put together while awake.

The cops cuff the monk and lead him away. All of a sudden, the Master wakes up, lets out a huge yawn, and stretches like he just had the best afternoon nap of his life.
> Huh? Everything wrapped up already? Where'd everybody go? Did I solve it in my sleep again? Well, what can I say, I'm the king! Nobody's on my level! Hahaha!

He bursts out into a loud, obnoxious laugh while his daughter just shakes her head and buries her face in her hands. Facepalm of the year.

Then... the little kid with the glasses quietly sidles up next to Tanaka. He takes one last look at the yellow tape, now just fluttering sadly in the wind. For a split second, Tanaka and the kid lock eyes. And for that one brief moment, there's this grave, grown-up look in the kid's eyes. Tanaka gets the uncanny feeling that this little squirt knows everything—that he's playing 4D chess with all of them, and they're just pawns on his board. It's almost eerie.

Then *snap*—a moment later the look vanishes, like someone flipped a switch. He's grinning again like any annoying little kid who only has candy on the brain.
> Hey big sis! I'm starving! I'm freezing! My feet are blocks of ice! Can we finally go get that famous sushi Uncle told us about?

The trio heads off toward town. Tanaka stands alone in the snow, adjusts his cap, and just thinks to himself:
> What the hell just happened? Am I even awake right now?

Hey, at least he's got one hell of a story to tell his grandkids someday. And now he knows one golden rule: whenever that guy with the mustache falls asleep, you'd better listen closely.

*Disclaimer: Obviously the story is made up, folks—a little homage to my favorite detective series. The photo is real, though, and the vibe in Otaru really is that mystical.*
