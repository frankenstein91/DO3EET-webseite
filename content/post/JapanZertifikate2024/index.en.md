There is plenty to collect on a trip to Japan. It's not just about the usual photos and memories—Japan is also full of gashapon, stamps, stickers, and certificates. On my last trip in November 2024, I tackled three of these wonderfully quirky certificates.
<!--more-->
# Charming Visitor Certificate (Fuji)
The Charming Visitor Certificate is basically a souvenir certificate you receive when you visit certain spots around Mount Fuji and the mountain "shyly" hides behind the clouds because of your "beauty." It's the size of a postcard and printed in both Japanese and English. The card gets stamped with the current date and bears the mayor's signature.

# Kanmon TOPPA! Commemorative Certificate
Another feat is walking through the Kanmon Pedestrian Tunnel. This tunnel runs underneath the ocean from one island to the next. On each side, there is half of a stamp. If you take the completed stamp on paper to one of the tourist information centers and ask for the certificate, they'll issue you this postcard-sized proof of your achievement.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanZertifikate2024/Kanmon-Cert.png" alt="Kanmon TOPPA! Commemorative Certificate" width="1000" height="1000" >}}

Quite the achievement, especially for anyone suffering from claustrophobia.

# Tempozan Mountaineering Certificate
Osaka also gives you the chance to get your accomplishments certified. This certificate is for reaching the summit of a mountain near the Osaka Aquarium. Fortunately, nobody bothered to define what counts as an accomplishment, so you can feel proud of yourself here too. After conquering the peak—an unimaginable feat of endurance—you can take a proof-of-summit selfie to nearby restaurants to claim your certificate. We went with a takoyaki shop near the station.

The sheet is the size of two postcards and can be cut down the middle with scissors. One half is a postcard, and the other half is the double-sided certificate, also written in both English and Japanese.
