From the maritime high-tech district of Odaiba to the tradition-steeped alleyways of Asakusa: day three of our trip was all about water—and a hotel transfer unlike any other. Instead of wrestling suitcases through commuter-packed subway cars, we picked arguably the most stylish route in Tokyo: aboard a futuristic anime cruiser cutting across the bay.

It turned into a day full of awesome shifts in perspective: a morning ship ballet right outside our hotel, dizzying panoramas from the giant sphere of Fuji TV headquarters, a successful "rematch" on a footbridge with strict opening hours, and a charming streak of minor tech-maintenance hiccups—topped off with barefoot tatami bliss in an onsen and a fairytale light display at Ushijima Shrine.

<!--more-->

## Morning Ship Ballet Off Harumi: The Mitsui Ocean Sakura

Before packing up and checking out of our hotel in Toyosu, walking through the glass-walled elevator lobby treated us to a top-tier nautical show.

Far below in the harbor basin off Harumi Pier, a huge white cruise ship—the brand-new **Mitsui Ocean Sakura** (the only luxury cruise ship registered in Tokyo)—was executing a millimeter-accurate turn.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-TokioTag3/PXL_20261110_223526633.jpg" alt="The cruise ship Mitsui Ocean Sakura being turned by a tugboat in the harbor basin off Harumi" width="1200" height="675" >}}

Within tight quarters between the seawall and the modern residential towers of Harumi Flag, a nimble green-and-white harbor tug pivoted the ocean giant around its own axis with remarkable patience and precision. Watching the interplay of sheer mass, rumbling engines, and delicate tugboat teamwork felt almost meditative—the ideal maritime opening act for the day.

After checking out, we stashed our bags at the Toyosu hotel front desk for a couple of hours: why haul luggage when you can explore morning Odaiba completely unburdened?

Instead of hopping straight on the train, we set off on foot: following the waterfront path and crossing *Fujimi Bridge* (富士見橋—literally "Fuji-View Bridge"), we strolled toward Odaiba. And the bridge certainly lived up to its name in spectacular fashion: in the crisp, crystal-clear morning air, we were treated to a jaw-dropping view of snow-capped **Mount Fuji**, rising majestically right beside the Rainbow Bridge and the Minato skyline!

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-TokioTag3/PXL_20261110_230540069.jpg" alt="Crystal-clear view across Tokyo Bay and the Rainbow Bridge toward snow-capped Mount Fuji" width="1200" height="675" >}}

Postcard moments like that don't come along every day even for seasoned Tokyo regulars—the sacred mountain showed off its absolute best angle right before we reached Odaiba's sprawling waterfront.

## Futuristic Architecture in Odaiba: Up Into Fuji TV's "Hachitama" Sphere

Odaiba is a mecca for futuristic 1990s utopias, and no structure embodies that spirit more boldly than the monumental **Fuji Television** headquarters designed by star architect Kenzō Tange. Out on the plaza, visitors are greeted by the network's blue mascot "Laugh-kun" (*Rafu-kun*) beside a spherical fountain:

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-TokioTag3/PXL_20261111_001931277.jpg" alt="Plaza outside the Fuji TV building with the spherical fountain and blue Laugh-kun mascots" width="1200" height="675" >}}

The building's real centerpiece, however, hangs suspended high in the air: the titanium-clad, 32-meter-wide observation sphere known as **Hachitama** (*Hachitama Observatory*), slung dramatically between the two main towers on the 25th floor.

From up there, a sweeping 270-degree panorama opens out across all of Tokyo Bay. The *Rainbow Bridge* arcs gracefully over deep blue water toward the Minato skyline, while in the background red-and-white Tokyo Tower rises above the skyscrapers as a reassuring landmark:

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-TokioTag3/PXL_20261111_011835833.jpg" alt="Panoramic view from Fuji TV's Hachitama sphere overlooking the Rainbow Bridge and the skyline with Tokyo Tower" width="1200" height="675" >}}

The view east and south over the docks is fascinating too: zoomed in with a telephoto lens, the bright white hull of the classic car ferry **Sunflower** stood out at the ferry terminal with its unmistakable red sun graphic, backed on the horizon by the bold steel trusses of the *Tokyo Gate Bridge*:

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-TokioTag3/PXL_20261111_015735708.jpg" alt="Telephoto shot from Fuji TV showing the Sunflower ferry in the harbor and Tokyo Gate Bridge on the horizon" width="1200" height="675" >}}

## Yoshinoya Fuel and Riding the "People Mover" Back to Our Bags

After all that high-altitude air and harbor gazing, our stomachs chimed in. In Odaiba we popped into **Yoshinoya** for a traditional, wonderfully fuss-free lunch—a steaming bowl of juicy *gyūdon* (beef over rice) is simply the ultimate Japanese classic when you want something fast, cheap, and reliably delicious.

Well fed, it was time to retrieve our suitcases from the hotel. For the ride back to Toyosu, we took the fully automated **Yurikamome** line. As a tech and transit nerd, driverless rubber-tired guideway trains like this immediately make me think of the term **"People Mover"** (wonderfully geeked out over in [Technology Connections' video](https://youtu.be/Q2a9Yvo2Yyg?si=TQDr0Vs-WKvzd_NE) on Disney's Texas PeopleMover system). Running along its elevated concrete track, the computer-controlled train weaves effortlessly between futuristic towers—hard to imagine a smoother, more relaxing ride back.

In Toyosu we picked up our bags from the front desk and rode the People Mover four stops back to *Odaiba-Kaihinkōen*. From there it was just an easy five-minute walk down to the pier at *Odaiba Seaside Park*.

## Boarding at 13:18: Aboard the "Queen Emeraldas" to Asakusa

For our hotel transfer to Asakusa, we had booked the 13:18 boat departure ahead of time.

On this route, *Tokyo Cruise* operates its famous vessels designed by anime and manga legend **Leiji Matsumoto** (*Galaxy Express 999*, *Space Battleship Yamato*)—*Himiko*, *Hotaluna*, and *Emeraldas*. Which exact ship pulls up to the dock stays a little surprise right up until boarding.

When the boat glided in, every sci-fi fan's heart skipped a beat: it was **Queen Emeraldas** herself!

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-TokioTag3/PXL_20261111_040145757.jpg" alt="Front view of the futuristic sci-fi boat Queen Emeraldas docked at Odaiba Seaside Park" width="1200" height="675" >}}

With its sleek, silvery lines, sweeping panoramic glass, and teardrop cockpit, Queen Emeraldas looks far less like a riverboat and much more like a starship that accidentally touched down on Tokyo Bay.

Inside, it leans all the way into the spacefaring fantasy too: gull-wing doors lift open for boarding, onboard announcements are voiced by the original Matsumoto anime voice actors, and the cockpit looks straight out of a classic sci-fi bridge.

Once the vessel cleared the sheltered waters of Odaiba Bay, the open-air rooftop observation deck opened up:

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-TokioTag3/PXL_20261111_042219816.jpg" alt="Riding on the open rooftop deck of Queen Emeraldas across Tokyo Bay toward the skyline" width="1200" height="675" >}}

Standing out in the breeze atop the ship's aerodynamic spine as we glided beneath the towering pylons of the Rainbow Bridge with the Tokyo skyline scrolling past was an unforgettable experience.

Along the way, we spotted the full colorful spectrum of Tokyo watercraft: alongside standard tour boats, a neon-pink pleasure cruiser covered in white polka dots named "Azuma" suddenly crossed our bow:

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-TokioTag3/PXL_20261111_043053372~2.jpg" alt="A bright pink excursion boat with white polka dots crossing our path in the harbor" width="1200" height="675" >}}

Shortly afterward, just as we entered the Sumida River, we got the ultimate sci-fi rendezvous on the water: sister ship **Hotaluna** swept gracefully past us—two nautical works of art by Leiji Matsumoto crossing paths right in the middle of the river:

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-TokioTag3/PXL_20261111_043556724.jpg" alt="Passing sister ship Hotaluna on the Sumida River in front of riverside buildings" width="1200" height="675" >}}

Around 14:05, we finally docked at Asakusa Pier. Stepping ashore greets you with two of the city's boldest icons: the 634-meter *Tokyo Skytree* and Philippe Starck's golden flame (*Flamme d'Or*) atop the Asahi Brewery headquarters:

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-TokioTag3/PXL_20261111_050521842.jpg" alt="View from Asakusa Pier of Tokyo Skytree, the Asahi Beer Headquarters, and the golden flame" width="1200" height="675" >}}

From here it was only a short walk to our home for the next few days, *Onyado Nono Asakusa*, to effortlessly drop off the bags.

## Sumida River Walk: Our "Rematch" and the Sakura Rail Strike

Light-headed and luggage-free, we set off on a long afternoon wander through autumn Asakusa. First item on the agenda: our very own little "rematch" at the **Sumida River Walk**!

This pedestrian walkway, bolted directly onto the Tobu Skytree Line railway bridge, links Asakusa straight across to Sumida Park on the east bank. The catch with this handy shortcut: strict opening hours (07:00 to 22:00). Show up too late at night and you're staring at locked gates—a lesson we triumphantly put behind us in broad daylight today.

On the east bank, we strolled through Sumida Park to historic *Ushijima Shrine* (famous for its lucky cow statues), followed the riverbank, and crossed back to the west bank over the distinctive X-shaped *Sakurabashi* pedestrian bridge.

Right behind it rises a truly unique Buddhist sanctuary: **Matsuchiyama Shoden** (*Honryū-in*). Perched atop a small hill, the temple is dedicated to Kangiten, and its signature motif jumps right out at you: everywhere on the grounds you'll see carvings and offerings of giant white radishes (*daikon*), symbolizing physical health, purification, and family harmony.

To spare older visitors the steep climb up the hillside stairs, the slope has a tiny, cabin-style funicular affectionately named **Sakura Rail** (*さくらレール*).

Yet one glance at the lower station had us bursting out laughing:

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-TokioTag3/PXL_20261111_063921055.jpg" alt="Barrier gate blocking the Sakura Rail funicular at Matsuchiyama Shoden Temple with an out-of-service maintenance sign" width="1200" height="675" >}}

A hefty barrier blocked the entrance, sporting a freshly taped sign: **点検中** (*Tenken-chū*—"Closed for maintenance")!

Following the CVE lottery bad luck at the fish market and the out-of-service Ghibli clock the day before, our streak of minor technical absurdities held strong on day three as well. Japan clearly takes its safety inspections seriously—so we tackled the handful of stairs on foot like good sports.

## Hidden Paths: Sanyabori Park and the Mysterious Tobi Fudō

From Matsuchiyama Shoden, we followed the long, idyllic **Sanyabori Park** (*Sanyabori Kōen*). Built over a filled-in canal that once carried pleasure boats to the historic Yoshiwara entertainment district, the paved path is now lined with charming *Imado-ware* ceramics (famous as one of the birthplaces of the *maneki-neko* beckoning cat) and shrine foxes (*Teppō Kitsune*).

At the northern end, we turned into the quiet residential lanes of Ryūsen and stumbled upon a peaceful pocket untouched by tourist crowds: **Tobi Fudōson** (*Ryūkōzan Shōbō-in*).

Without even realizing it at first, we had wandered into a temple with a wonderfully quirky claim to fame: it is renowned across Japan as a guardian sanctuary for **aviation safety and safe travels**! According to legend, in the 16th century the statue of protector deity Fudō Myōō flew back to Tokyo overnight from a distant pilgrimage to answer believers' prayers—earning him the nickname *Tobi Fudō* ("the Flying Fudō"). Today, airline pilots, cabin crew, aerospace engineers, and frequent flyers make pilgrimages here to pick up special flight charms (*hikō-mamori*).

In the quiet courtyard, the serene atmosphere completely charmed us: shaded by a bamboo grove, two masterfully carved stone Rakan statues (disciples of Buddha) sit deep in conversation beneath a bright red pagoda lantern:

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-TokioTag3/PXL_20261111_071620189.jpg" alt="Two cheerful stone Rakan statues chatting under a red lantern in the garden of Tobi Fudō Temple" width="600" height="1067" >}}

Their mischievous, expressive faces radiated a cheerful calm that felt wonderfully soothing after the buzz of Odaiba and Asakusa.

## Checking Into Onyado Nono: Barefoot on Tatami

Around 17:00 we returned to our hotel for proper check-in: **Onyado Nono Asakusa** (*御宿 野乃 浅草*).

Anyone familiar with Dormy Inn's brands knows the special comfort factor here: the moment you step past the front door, it's shoes off and into a locker! The entire hotel—from hallways and elevators to every single guest room—is carpeted wall-to-wall in real, springy tatami mats.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-TokioTag3/PXL_20261111_080002794.jpg" alt="Traditional-meets-modern hotel room at Onyado Nono Asakusa with tatami flooring and shoji sliding screens" width="1200" height="675" >}}

After logging so many kilometers on Tokyo asphalt, walking barefoot or in socks across fragrant rush grass feels like pure luxury.

The crowning touch, however, lies downstairs: the in-house onsen draws from Tokyo's famous **Kuroyu** (literally "black water")—an amber-to-inky-dark mineral spring water that feels wonderfully silky on the skin and revives tired muscles in no time. After soaking, the brand's legendary perks awaited: free ice cream outside the baths and the promise of a steaming bowl of *Yonaki Soba* (Dormy Inn's complimentary late-night ramen).

## Magical Finale: Lights at Ushijima Shrine

The evening still held one final showstopper in store: after a brief rest, we headed out for a short night stroll—back across the Sumida River Walk to **Ushijima Shrine**.

What awaited us there was a scene we won't forget anytime soon: as part of the winter light festival **Tokyo Shitamachi Kaiyū Take-akari** (東京下町回遊 竹あかり), the entire shrine precinct was bathed in a warm sea of light.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-TokioTag3/PXL_20261111_093414837.jpg" alt="Fairytale illumination with bamboo lanterns and colorful glowing spheres at Ushijima Shrine in front of the lit-up Skytree" width="600" height="1067" >}}

Along the stone-paved path, bamboo stalks drilled with intricate filigree patterns (*take-akari*) glowed softly. Overhead floated a canopy of colorful, geometrically woven light spheres in vivid blue, green, yellow, and red.

And framed squarely down the central axis above the torii gate and trees rose the illuminated **Tokyo Skytree**, its spire gleaming against the night sky. A picture straight out of a dream—the flawless harmony of ancient Shintō tradition, delicate handcraft, and cutting-edge architecture.

A rich day spanning the bay, manga aesthetics, quiet temple gardens, and glowing landmarks winds down. Tokyo couldn't have cast a finer spell on us today!
