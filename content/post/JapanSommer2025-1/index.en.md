I waited a long time for this, and now it's finally happening again: I'm back in Japan! The country that never fails to fascinate me has me in its grip once more. Getting here took a while, sure, yet it was also the perfect prelude to a brand-new adventure in the Land of the Rising Sun.  
The journey kicked off smoothly enough in Leipzig with a short hop over to Frankfurt. Waiting there (tucked away in some hangar somewhere) was the bird that would carry me halfway across the globe: an ANA (All Nippon Airways) Dreamliner. Just spotting it at the gate always triggers that special kind of pre-trip buzz. The long-haul flight itself turned out surprisingly pleasant—after about a four-hour layover in Frankfurt, which I spent stretching my legs and soaking in the international airport vibe.  
And once again, ANA didn't disappoint on the food front! Dinner was a teriyaki-style chicken with veggies, edamame, and of course impeccably cooked Japanese rice. Simple, yet genuinely delicious—a great preview of Japanese cuisine. Overall the flight was super smooth, save for one brief patch of light turbulence—a quick wobble high above the clouds that passed in no time. Most of the night, the Dreamliner just glided effortlessly through the dark.

After what felt like forever and yet no time at all, we touched down in Tokyo. That moment when you step out of the air-conditioned terminal and take your very first breath of humid Japanese air is hard to put into words. Once through immigration, it was—how could it be anything else—straight onto the train into the city. That first ride on Tokyo's dense yet impossibly punctual rail network always feels like a mini "welcome home."

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanSommer2025-1/PXL_20250820_002637195.jpg" alt="" width="1000" height="1000" >}}

With my bags stashed at the hotel, nothing could stop me from wandering out to explore the neighborhood. My very first stroll led straight to a few neat discoveries. Just steps from the hotel, I stumbled across an impressive stone statue of a cheerfully grinning deity perched on sacks of rice/money with a mallet in hand—likely Daikokuten, one of the Seven Lucky Gods. Not a bad opening greeting from Japanese culture!

The shrine grounds themselves are a lovely little oasis of calm. The main hall with its vivid red timber and sweeping turquoise roof looks striking, and the hush across the courtyard offered a welcome breather from the city's buzz—inviting you to pause for a quiet moment.

## Evening Glow on the Rainbow Bridge

After soaking up some culture by day, evening pulled me toward a spot famous for its views: the Rainbow Bridge. Strolling along the walkway is the ideal way to watch Tokyo's legendary sea of lights come alive. As the sky slowly shifted from blue to soft violet, the city flickered on, laying out an incredible panorama of lit-up office towers and busy harbor traffic.  
The later it got, the more the scene turned into a sparkling ocean of millions of lights dancing in reflections across Tokyo Bay. Watching those glittering skyscrapers never gets old—it's one of those moments that sticks with you.

The walk across the bridge and the glowing skyline had me so hooked that I decided to keep rambling on a bit further. Honestly, I had aimed for the well-known Odaiba Marine Park, only for my inner explorer to accidentally wander into neighboring Daiba Park instead. Still, as so often in Japan, the best finds are the ones you didn't plan!

That little "wrong turn" turned out to be pure gold. From the park, another fantastic angle across the bay opened up—highlighted by the futuristic Fuji Television building, whose giant suspended sphere looks lifted straight out of a sci-fi movie.

The whole waterfront was humming with life: out on the bay, traditional *yakatabune* dinner boats traced slow, glowing trails across the water, adding a warm counterpoint to the cool steel-and-glass skyline.

After a **first night** in Tokyo following that awesome evening by the bay, today promised a full slate of exploring. My schedule was packed, and I couldn't wait to dive right back into the city's many layers.

## Inside the World of Photography: The Nikon Museum

The morning started off technical and precise—fitting enough for Japan. My target was the Nikon Museum, an essential pilgrimage for anyone with even a passing interest in photography. Over a century of history from one of the world's best-known camera makers comes alive here, from historic milestone bodies to cutting-edge digital marvels. Seeing how the tech evolved across the decades—and the sheer engineering prowess packed into those cameras—was fascinating.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanSommer2025-1/PXL_20250821_011410370.jpg" alt="" width="1000" height="1000" >}}

After that much hardware, it was time to refuel—and few spots in Tokyo beat the Tsukiji Outer Market for the job. Even though the famous wholesale auction moved away, the outer market hasn't lost an ounce of its charm. The narrow lanes overflow with energy, aromas, and flavors, packed with tiny stalls and eateries dishing up fresh-off-the-boat seafood and countless Japanese specialties. I treated myself to a phenomenal lunch—you really can't get fish any fresher than this. An absolute must for any Japan fan and a feast for the taste buds.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanSommer2025-1/PXL_20250821_030419319.jpg" alt="" width="1000" height="1000" >}}

The afternoon was all about contrast. Right in the middle of ultra-modern Shiodome, hemmed in by towering skyscrapers, lies an oasis of peace: Hama-rikyū Gardens. This traditional Japanese landscape garden is simply gorgeous—tidal seawater ponds, picturesque island teahouses, and impeccably sculpted pines framed against shimmering high-rises. It's exactly this kind of juxtaposition that makes Tokyo so special.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanSommer2025-1/PXL_20250821_043741657.LONG_EXPOSURE-01.COVER.jpg" alt="" width="1000" height="1000" >}}

Just a short walk away waited another gem: Kyū-Shiba-Rikyū Garden. One of Tokyo's oldest gardens, it radiates a remarkably quiet, almost meditative calm. Looping around the central pond past little hills, pebble beaches, and stone lanterns, it's easy to forget for a moment that you're standing inside one of the biggest cities on Earth.
