With my APRS radio and my e-Golf, I set out on the road for a one-of-a-kind outing. What made this excursion truly special, though, was the rather unconventional antenna setup I chose. My goal for the day was to test the range of my APRS transceiver while enjoying some gorgeous scenery—and then tinker with this website a bit once I got back.
<!--more-->

For anyone wondering what APRS is: Automatic Packet Reporting System enables the automatic transmission of data like GPS coordinates, weather telemetry, and text messages over the radio network. It's a fascinating technology used primarily by amateur radio operators. Think of it as very similar to SMS or RCS—just without the RCS encryption.

To pull this off, however, I first had to come up with a workable antenna mount. Luckily, a creative idea struck me: a photography tripod could make the perfect support for my antenna. After packing up my e-Golf and getting the APRS radio ready, I strapped the antenna securely (with rubber bands) to a camera tripod, and then placed the tripod on top of the car roof. Using a tripod as an improvised antenna mast seemed downright brilliant—and full of promise.

My packets got out nicely from that antenna perch and reached the digipeater DM0RLB-10—meaning theoretical contact with fellow radio globetrotters was established. I also gave sending emails over APRS another test run.

All in all, the day was a great mix of technical curiosity, driving fun, and creative problem-solving. It didn't just let me explore what my APRS rig can do; it also showed that a bit of creativity and improvisation can go a surprisingly long way. Sometimes all you need is a camera tripod and the willingness to look at things from a different angle.
