Strictly speaking, the new day begins in the middle of the night — just after 1 a.m., I am still sitting at the desk in my room at **Dormy Inn Gifu Ekimae**, laptop glowing, sorting photos and writing up blog notes. After yesterday's steady rain, the forecast finally promises clear blue skies over Gifu.

## Morning Among Inaba Shrine, Zenkō-ji, and the Great Buddha

Rested and ready, the morning begins on foot and by local bus toward **Inaba Shrine (伊奈波神社)**. Below the main halls, a quiet little waterfall spills beside a stone lantern, which makes a great excuse for a handheld long exposure. A little further along sits the smaller **Fukutoku Kokuryū Ōkami (福徳黒龍大神)** shrine with its stone torii, lanterns, and racks of wooden *ema* plaques.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKinkazan/PXL_20261116_004511245.LONG_EXPOSURE-01.jpg" alt="Long exposure of a small waterfall beside a stone lantern at Inaba Shrine" width="600" height="1067" >}}

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKinkazan/PXL_20261116_004813214.jpg" alt="Stone torii, lanterns, and ema plaques at the Fukutoku Kokuryū Ōkami shrine within Inaba Shrine" width="1200" height="675" >}}

Right next door stands **Gifu Inaba Zenkō-ji (岐阜・伊奈波 善光寺)**, its dark wooden main hall gleaming in the crisp morning sun beneath a bright blue sky streaked with a single contrail.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKinkazan/PXL_20261116_010326864.jpg" alt="Main hall of Gifu Inaba Zenkō-ji under a clear blue morning sky" width="1200" height="675" >}}

Just a few minutes away, **Shōhō-ji (正法寺)** holds one of the morning's biggest highlights. In the peaceful courtyard, pine branches frame a weathered stone lantern, and right behind it a tall **Hōkyōintō stone pagoda (宝篋印塔)** rises against the blue sky. With delicate chains and tiny bells hanging down from the spire to its upturned corners, it looks like a remarkably ornate stone lantern at first glance — an easy detail to fall in love with.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKinkazan/PXL_20261116_012008069.jpg" alt="Stone lantern framed by pine branches in the courtyard of Shōhō-ji" width="600" height="1067" >}}

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKinkazan/PXL_20261116_012906627.jpg" alt="Hōkyōintō stone pagoda rising against a clear blue sky at Shōhō-ji" width="600" height="1067" >}}

Inside the main hall sits the **Gifu Great Buddha (岐阜大仏)**, towering **13.7 meters** tall. The cross-section diagram displayed inside makes its construction especially fascinating: unlike cast-bronze statues, it is built around a massive central wooden **Great Pillar** using woven **bamboo**, shaped with **clay**, layered with **sutra paper**, sealed in **lacquer**, and finished with **gold leaf**. Seeing it in the quiet dimness of the hall makes the craftsmanship all the more impressive.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKinkazan/PXL_20261116_013927288.jpg" alt="The 13.7-meter-tall Gifu Great Buddha inside the hall of Shōhō-ji" width="600" height="1067" >}}

## A Brick Face, Historic Kawaramachi, and the Ukai Museum

Walking toward the Nagara River, an unexpected piece of canal infrastructure catches my eye: an old brick sluice gate whose two pointed metal caps on top and round porthole openings underneath look unmistakably like a **cat or retro robot face**.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKinkazan/PXL_20261116_023404034.jpg" alt="Old brick sluice gate whose pointed metal caps and round openings resemble a cat or robot face" width="600" height="1067" >}}

Nearby, a stone dragon guards a flat world-map disc surrounded by carved waves — if Flat-Earthers ever needed visual proof, Gifu apparently already has the whole disc ready complete with a resident dragon. A few steps further near Minatomachi, a bronze statue of a traditional **ushō** (cormorant fisherman) stands with his bird and woven basket.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKinkazan/PXL_20261116_023616346.jpg" alt="Stone dragon perched on a flat carved world-map disc above sculpted waves" width="600" height="1067" >}}

Shortly afterward I step into the preserved historic district of **Kawaramachi (川原町)** at the foot of Mt. Kinka. By a wooden **Kōshin-dō (庚申堂)** post sits a little stone monkey in a red bib resting its chin in its hands, before the street opens into rows of traditional dark timber storefronts and lattice windows. Naturally, there is also a penguin-like *ushō* face-in-hole photo standee along the way.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKinkazan/PXL_20261116_030927480.jpg" alt="Face-in-hole photo standee of a mascot dressed as a cormorant fisherman" width="600" height="1067" >}}

Crossing the river brings me to the **Nagaragawa Ukai Museum (長良川うかいミュージアム)**. Outside the modern glass building stand a bronze cormorant with outstretched wings and a stone replica of a wooden viewing boat. Inside, I check out the exhibition dedicated to Gifu's centuries-old **cormorant fishing (*Ukai*)** tradition along the Nagara River. At the same time, it feels like the exhibition could still use a bit more love and polish to make the subject genuinely engaging for international visitors as well.

## Shinshō-ji, Quiet Gifu Park, and Wooden Restroom Dividers

Heading back toward the park, I pass the wooden bell gate of **Shinshō-ji (真性寺)** before entering **Gifu Park (岐阜公園)** in the early afternoon. Its Japanese pond garden offers stone bridges, subtle hints of red autumn maple, and a heavily moss-covered stone lantern tucked beside a mossy tree trunk.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKinkazan/PXL_20261116_042632642.jpg" alt="Wooden temple bell gate of Shinshō-ji in Gifu" width="600" height="1067" >}}

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKinkazan/PXL_20261116_052451411.jpg" alt="Japanese pond garden in Gifu Park with stone bridge and stone lantern" width="1200" height="675" >}}

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKinkazan/PXL_20261116_052500292.jpg" alt="Close-up of a heavily moss-covered stone lantern in Gifu Park" width="600" height="1067" >}}

Rising bright red against the wooded hillside above the park stands the **Three-storied Pagoda of Gifu Park (岐阜公園三重塔)**. As two workers attend to the small wooden gate at the top of the stone steps behind the bamboo lattice fence, it is well worth pausing for the story behind this **Registered Tangible Cultural Property**: funded with contributions from the citizens of Gifu City, the pagoda was erected on **November 21, 1917** to commemorate Emperor Taishō's ascension to the throne.

Set atop a two-tiered stone base, the three-storied wooden structure even incorporates salvaged timber from the old **Nagara Bridge**, which collapsed on **October 28, 1891** during the **Great Mino Earthquake**. Its deliberately restrained, unornamented traditional design comes from **Chuta Ito** — the architect who also designed Tokyo's Meiji Shrine and is regarded as the founder of the historical study of Japanese architecture. One particularly intriguing detail shown in the cross-section diagram on the sign at the site is tucked inside: the central heart pillar hangs suspended by chains just above the foundation stone, an unusual structural method found in only a handful of pagodas from the late Edo to Meiji periods. Even the location itself carries a personal touch: it is said to have been chosen by **Gyokudo Kawai**, a celebrated Japanese painter whose hometown was Gifu City.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKinkazan/PXL_20261116_053247088.jpg" alt="The red Three-storied Pagoda of Gifu Park seen in daylight" width="600" height="1067" >}}

And keeping up my streak of noticing quirky everyday details, a stop inside Gifu Park's public restroom deserves its own highlight. What immediately catches the eye is the whole package: set between rustic **light natural-wood slatted dividers**, the urinals have a delightfully odd, ringed **tub- or barrel-like cylindrical shape** standing all the way down on the floor — a floor-mounted design rarely seen in Europe. On top of that, even in a busy public park, everything from the dark stone floor to the tiled walls is **spotlessly clean**, accompanied by polite four-language signs from Gifu City (*"Thank you for keeping our toilets clean / Please stand close to the toilet to use cleanly"*). Especially compared to what one often expects from public park restrooms back in Europe, the level of cleanliness and thoughtful design leaves a genuinely lasting impression.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKinkazan/PXL_20261116_053929659.jpg" alt="Spotless public restroom in Gifu Park featuring floor-standing barrel-shaped urinals, natural wooden slatted dividers, and Gifu City cleanliness signs" width="600" height="1067" >}}

## Up Mt. Kinka by Ropeway: From Afternoon Sun to a Sea of Lights

In the afternoon I ride the **Mt. Kinka Ropeway** up the forested mountainside. Anyone heading up the mountain right now quickly notices why the focus is strictly on the viewpoints rather than the castle itself: **Gifu Castle** is in the middle of a long-term seismic reinforcement and renovation project. Its main keep has been closed and wrapped in scaffolding since **May 19, 2026** (the nearby archives museum since April 2026), with a reopening not scheduled until around **November 2027**. The **Mt. Kinka hiking trails** are similarly affected by long-term summit and pathway construction running through roughly **late September 2027**: several routes (including *Meisō no Komichi* / Mizute Trail and the Hanataka course) currently hit dead ends before the summit, and the area immediately around the castle keep is closed off. Fortunately, the **Mt. Kinka Ropeway** and the summit observation decks remain open — and looking down from the passing white gondola, patches of orange and red autumn foliage already dot the steep evergreen slopes.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKinkazan/PXL_20261116_064423951.PANO.jpg" alt="Wide afternoon panorama from Mt. Kinka overlooking Gifu City and the winding Nagara River" width="1200" height="419" >}}

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKinkazan/PXL_20261116_064715146.jpg" alt="View from Mt. Kinka across forested mountain ridges into the valley" width="1200" height="675" >}}

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKinkazan/PXL_20261116_070054011.jpg" alt="Mt. Kinka Ropeway gondola gliding above the forested slope with autumn colors" width="1200" height="675" >}}

From the summit observation deck, I decide to stay put and watch afternoon fade into twilight. As the sun sinks behind the distant mountain ridge, the clouds catch warm pink and orange tones during **blue hour**, the **Nagara River** reflects the last light of the sky, and thousands of city lights flicker to life across the plain. Only after full nightfall settles in — with even a tiny green-lit Ferris wheel glowing far in the distance — do I ride the ropeway back down.

## Illuminated Pagoda, Umbrellas Around Nobunaga, and City Light Trails

Already on the nighttime descent on the **Mt. Kinka Ropeway**, a great shot appears straight from inside the gondola: down in **Gifu Park**, the **Three-storied Pagoda** glows in warm spotlights against the dark trees and night sky — a striking contrast to just a few hours earlier. One amusing detail stands out on this ride in the dark as well: the ropeway gondolas actually have **headlights** in front and **red taillights** at the back — almost like a car suspended from a cable.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKinkazan/PXL_20261116_083324697.jpg" alt="Photographed from the Mt. Kinka Ropeway gondola: the illuminated red Three-storied Pagoda of Gifu Park at night" width="600" height="1067" >}}

At **JR Gifu Station**, another night display awaits out on the plaza: the golden statue of **Oda Nobunaga** stands above a glowing semicircle of colorful traditional **Gifu *wagasa* (paper umbrellas)** alongside illuminated lanterns and carp artwork.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKinkazan/PXL_20261116_090439615.jpg" alt="Golden Oda Nobunaga statue outside JR Gifu Station surrounded by illuminated traditional paper umbrellas" width="1200" height="675" >}}

Right next to the station, I head up to the free observation floor in **Gifu City Tower 43**. Looking straight down at the avenues and the illuminated pedestrian bridges spanning the station plaza, long-exposure shots turn the evening traffic into crisp streaks of red and white **light trails**.
