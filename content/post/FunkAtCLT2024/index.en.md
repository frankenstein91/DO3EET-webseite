After a wonderful weekend at the [Chemnitzer Linux-Tage](https://chemnitzer.linux-tage.de/2024/de), "normal" life is kicking back into gear. Still, I wanted to share a quick recap of the event.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/FunkAtCLT2024/PXL_20240319_051906998.jpg" alt="Birds on the antenna" width="800" height="600" >}}

Many folks had already met up the day before to set up the venue and catch up. After finishing my day job, I only had a few setup tasks left and went to check out my volunteer posts. Since I didn't have a [YaCy](https://github.com/yacy) booth this year, I had signed up to help with the streaming team and the "Dr. Tux's Practice"[^1] tech clinic.

On Friday, we got a quick crash course on the Chaos Computer Club's streaming tool so we could hit the ground running over the weekend. Using Voctomix, it was super easy to control and monitor the livestreams for everyone watching online.

Over at the nerd ER "Dr. Tux's Practice," we managed to untangle some pretty interesting problems too.

The event also offered plenty of opportunities to chat with fellow radio amateurs and check out several projects up close. "Ham Radio in Action" had spaces on both the left and right sides of the building. To the right of the entrance, projects like Winlink and FT8 were on display, while the other side showcased voice contacts via the QO-100 geostationary amateur radio satellite. Naturally, I had my handheld transceiver with me the whole time, listening in on the local repeater / SachsenLink from my hotel room in the evenings—and I fired off quite a few APRS beacons that were successfully picked up around Chemnitz.


[^1]: not an actual doctor, just a figure of speech
