Jingisukan (ジンギスカン), named after the Mongol conqueror Genghis Khan, is a Japanese lamb and mutton dish cooked on a special dome-shaped metal grill. It is a classic Hokkaido specialty.

# The Tabletop Experience
As you can see in the photo above, the whole thing is a very interactive affair:
* **The Dome:** The meat goes right on the peak of the grill.
* **The Drippings:** Fat and meat juices run down into the recessed rim of the pan.
* **The Veggies:** Down there sit mountains of *moyashi* (bean sprouts) and other vegetables, cooking in the meat juices and soaking up an insanely rich flavor.

# Why It Tastes So Good
Since I'm not a fan of honey or sweet mushy dishes, the savory, almost rustic kick of Jingisukan is right up my alley. The meat is often marinated, or dipped after grilling into a zesty soy-based sauce. In the crisp air of Toyako, there's hardly anything better than sitting in front of that sizzling grill.
