I originally wanted to keep politics off this blog and stick to ham radio, tech, and travel—but I'm breaking my own rule today to put my thoughts on paper about one particular topic. As a former participant in the [German-Japanese Young Professionals Exchange Program](https://jdzb.de/de/austauschprogramme/berufstaetige) at the Japanese-German Center Berlin (JDZB), where "work-life balance" was an ever-present theme, I keep a close eye on any shifts in how we talk about working hours.

Over the past few days, there's been plenty of debate around a proposal by our current Finance Minister Christian Lindner to make overtime pay tax-free. Even I—despite being a self-confessed workaholic with a hefty overtime balance—view this idea with a good deal of skepticism.

## What the Proposal Aims to Do
The Federal Ministry of Finance hopes to make working extra hours more attractive and thereby give the economy a boost—essentially banking on squeezing more output out of the existing workforce.

## Where the Idea Falls Short
Most employees are already motivated and do good work—sure, there are always exceptions. This proposal probably owes a lot to the media noise made by certain hustle-culture influencers, yet in practice it casts doubt on the work ethic of everyone already pulling their weight.

# My Counterproposal
Instead, policymakers should look at the eye-watering costs of professional training and upskilling, which are barely affordable for individual workers even on above-average salaries. A course on hardening Active Directory, for instance, easily costs €2,500—yet it's practically mandatory if you want to run that software in compliance with GDPR. More often than not, employers have to foot the bill, or attending as an individual is simply out of reach. On top of that, tax-free overtime would severely undermine instruments like lifetime working-time accounts (*Lebensarbeitszeitkonto*). Publicly subsidizing individual continuing education would, in my view, be far more effective at helping people handle their workload within regular working hours. And if even a team of highly motivated employees can't keep up with the load, then maybe the employer should consider hiring additional staff. That approach wouldn't just boost morale—it would actually do the labor market as a whole some good.

## What is a Lifetime Working-Time Account?
A lifetime working-time account lets employees bank overtime hours, parts of their salary, and bonuses toward taking extended paid leave later on. Contributions are deducted from gross pay, and income tax isn't settled until the time off is actually taken.
