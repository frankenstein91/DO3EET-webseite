After a night in Shizuoka, today was time for the next change of scenery: in the morning, the Tōkaidō Line carried me further west to Kakegawa—including checking into new digs at the local Dormy Inn rather than heading back in the evening.

What came together along the route between the castle gate, the Stained Glass Museum, Tatsuo Shrine, and the train station was a wonderfully eclectic mix of history, quirky encounters, and happy accidents: two garden gnomes complete with a red dragonfly in the castle park, a surprise stone Doraemon, the historic Hōtoku Library, a spontaneous *"Could be a Book cover"* shot on a stone bench—plus a downright delicious taco pizza and the castle lit up at night in dual-tone blue and white as an atmospheric finale.

<!--more-->

---

## Starting in Shizuoka and Heading On to Kakegawa

Before the luggage rolled back toward the station, the morning in Shizuoka began at a relaxed pace at the breakfast buffet: a colorful spread of *tamagoyaki*, pasta, grilled chicken, little sausages, steamed *nikuman* buns, Japanese curry with rice, crispy breaded bites, and a grilled *yaki-onigiri* laid a solid foundation for a day on the move.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-Kakegawa/PXL_20261113_225305252.jpg" alt="Hotel breakfast tray in Shizuoka with tamagoyaki, curry rice, nikuman, yaki-onigiri, and side dishes" width="1200" height="675" >}}

On the walk to the station, a particularly lovingly designed, colorful fire hydrant cover (消火栓) on the sidewalk caught my eye: front and center, the snow-capped cone of Mount Fuji, framed by waterways and traditional rooftops.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-Kakegawa/PXL_20261113_235604108.jpg" alt="Colorful Japanese fire hydrant cover in Shizuoka featuring Mount Fuji, a river, and traditional rooftops" width="1200" height="675" >}}

At 09:23, the onward journey departed on the Tōkaidō Line (Local bound for Hamamatsu) from Shizuoka Station, Platform 4. After 45 minutes and ten stops, the train pulled right on schedule into Kakegawa Station (Platform 3) at 10:08.

---

## Kakegawa Castle: Garden Gnomes with a Dragonfly, Distant Fuji Views, and the Dormy Inn in Sight

From the station, I headed north on foot toward Kakegawa Castle (掛川城). Even the massive two-story main gate welcomes visitors with dark timber, whitewashed plaster walls, and striking *shachihoko* roof ornaments—and beyond it lies the castle grounds. Things around the area weren't quite as peaceful as hoped today, though: downtown near the castle, swarms of cosplayers were out and about, unfortunately accompanied by very loud music.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-Kakegawa/PXL_20261114_013728604.jpg" alt="Traditional two-story wooden gate with white plaster facade and roof ornaments at Kakegawa Castle" width="1200" height="675" >}}

In a flowerbed inside the castle park, a charming little scene awaited that had nothing to do with Japanese castle architecture: nestled among purple blossoms stood two classic garden gnomes in green pointy hats—and perched right on top of one hat was a bright red dragonfly, completely unfazed even at close range.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-Kakegawa/PXL_20261114_015513520.jpg" alt="Two garden gnomes with green pointy hats amidst a purple flowerbed in Kakegawa Castle Park" width="1200" height="675" >}}

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-Kakegawa/PXL_20261114_015556241.jpg" alt="Close-up of a bright red dragonfly perched right on top of a garden gnome's green pointy hat" width="1200" height="675" >}}

Up from the castle's main keep, the view opened wide across the city rooftops and surrounding hills—and tucked between power pylons and mountain ridges, the faint blue silhouette of Mount Fuji actually reappeared on the horizon.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-Kakegawa/PXL_20261114_021721043.jpg" alt="View from Kakegawa Castle across the sea of rooftops and wooded hills to the distant silhouette of Mount Fuji" width="1200" height="675" >}}

Panning across town also revealed a handy landmark: rising right behind a parking garage decked out with castle battlements and tiled roofs was the Dormy Inn Kakegawa (*天然温泉 茶月の湯*)—our exact quarters for the coming night.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-Kakegawa/PXL_20261114_021807111.jpg" alt="View from Kakegawa Castle of the Dormy Inn Kakegawa behind a castle-themed parking garage" width="600" height="1067" >}}

Looking down the ruler-straight arterial road, I also grabbed a long-exposure shot from above, turning mid-morning traffic into soft streaks of motion.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-Kakegawa/PXL_20261114_022545059.LONG_EXPOSURE-01.jpg" alt="Long exposure from the castle heights down a straight road in Kakegawa with blurred traffic trails" width="600" height="1067" >}}

Seen from the outside, the white main keep of Kakegawa Castle perches impressively atop its dark stone base (*ishigaki*) high above the ramparts.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-Kakegawa/PXL_20261114_024108827.jpg" alt="Exterior view of the white main keep of Kakegawa Castle atop its steep stone wall" width="1200" height="675" >}}

After the quick loop around the castle grounds, I grabbed a *Custard Pudding* Seventeen Ice as a little mid-walk treat.

---

## A Riot of Color at the Kakegawa Stained Glass Museum

Right on the edge of the castle grounds, the Kakegawa Stained Glass Museum (*掛川市ステンドグラス美術館*) offered a vivid change of pace. Along a tall end wall, nine circular rose windows glowed above a three-part altar glass ensemble in rich blues, reds, and golds, their light reflecting softly across the polished floor.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-Kakegawa/PXL_20261114_033709158.jpg" alt="Tall wall of circular and rectangular stained-glass windows in the Kakegawa Stained Glass Museum with floor reflections" width="600" height="1067" >}}

## Tatsuo Shrine: Inari Torii and a Stone Doraemon

North of the castle, the walk continued to Tatsuo Shrine (龍尾神社). Beneath shady trees, a small Inari shrine (*稲荷神社*) greeted visitors first with vibrant vermilion torii gates and two fox statues (*kitsune*) dressed in pink bibs.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-Kakegawa/PXL_20261114_041324613.jpg" alt="Vermilion torii gates of the Inari shrine at Tatsuo Shrine with two kitsune fox statues in the foreground" width="600" height="1067" >}}

Right next to it, a completely unexpected figure brought a smile to my face: in front of a row of colorful lanterns, a solid granite-carved Doraemon cheerfully waved at visitors—pop culture and shrine tradition standing side by side as if it were the most natural thing in the world.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-Kakegawa/PXL_20261114_042427351.jpg" alt="Stone Doraemon statue waving in front of colorful lanterns at Tatsuo Shrine in Kakegawa" width="600" height="1067" >}}

## A Quiet Temple and the Spontaneous "Could Be a Book Cover" on the Old Castle Grounds

On the walk back toward downtown stood a quiet, slightly weathered temple hall with gilded door fittings behind an old iron gate.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-Kakegawa/PXL_20261114_045302030.jpg" alt="Historic wooden temple hall with gilded doors behind a weathered iron gate and stone posts" width="1200" height="675" >}}

Just two minutes later, on the old castle grounds, came perhaps the single punchiest photo of the day. Walking past a stone bench, I noticed a clean vertical crack running straight through its ribbed front edge—and the idea clicked instantly: after accidentally nailing a *"Could be an album cover"* shot in Sunpu Park yesterday, today brought its vertical counterpart as a "Could be a Book cover"—a clenched fist poised right over the fissure, as if one punch had split the stone in two.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-Kakegawa/PXL_20261114_045550932~2.jpg" alt="Clenched fist wearing a smartwatch directly over a vertical crack in a ribbed stone bench, framed vertically as a book-cover shot" width="600" height="1008" >}}

## Hōtoku Library, Ninomiya Kinjirō, and a Traditional Phone Booth

In the afternoon, the route wound past the historic *Dai-Nippon Hōtokusha* complex with its distinctively tiled, patina-aged Hōtoku Library (*報徳図書館*).

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-Kakegawa/PXL_20261114_052451027.jpg" alt="Historic Hōtoku Library building with aged brick facade and kanji lettering under a gray sky" width="600" height="1067" >}}

Out front stands a classic bronze statue of Ninomiya Kinjirō (二宮金次郎)—the famous late-Edo scholar traditionally depicted carrying a bundle of firewood on his back while reading a book as he walks.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-Kakegawa/PXL_20261114_052536896.jpg" alt="Bronze statue of Ninomiya Kinjirō carrying firewood on his back with an open book in his hands" width="600" height="1067" >}}

A few steps further by a bridge waited another gem of Japanese street furniture: a traditionally built wooden phone booth topped with a small tiled roof, stained-glass arched windows, a classic green coin payphone inside—and two little folding stools marked *"KK"* propped right against the exterior wall.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-Kakegawa/PXL_20261114_053104056.jpg" alt="Traditional wooden phone booth with a tiled roof, patterned window, and two small folding stools outside" width="600" height="1067" >}}

## Taco Pizza to Wind Down and Checking In at Kakegawa

By late afternoon, after all those kilometers on foot, hunger finally made itself known. Near the station I had a freshly baked taco pizza loaded with spiced ground beef, crisp lettuce, diced tomatoes, crunchy tortilla chips, and cheese sauce—and it hit the spot completely.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-Kakegawa/PXL_20261114_064221609.jpg" alt="Freshly served taco pizza on a wooden board topped with lettuce, diced tomatoes, tortilla chips, and cheese sauce" width="1200" height="675" >}}

There was no heading back to Shizuoka afterward: after checking into the Dormy Inn Kakegawa—the very hotel we'd spotted from the castle that morning—evening (around 18:00) brought one last striking view of the town's landmark. From the same angle as at noon, Kakegawa Castle now towered against a pitch-black night sky—one half bathed in deep blue, the other brightly lit in white.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-Kakegawa/PXL_20261114_090003178.jpg" alt="Kakegawa Castle at night in dual-tone illumination—half lit in blue and half in white—against a dark sky above illuminated ramparts" width="1200" height="675" >}}
