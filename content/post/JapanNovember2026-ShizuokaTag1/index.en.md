Moving base from the metropolis of Tokyo to Shizuoka Prefecture—yet the day got off to a much bumpier start than you'd ever expect from Japan's rail network. A noticeable Shinkansen delay triggered an involuntary heart-stopping moment at Shin-Fuji Station before smartphone GPS saved the day.

Once in Shizuoka, however, the contrast couldn't have been better: scale-model nerd bliss with a *Tachikoma* by the station, quiet Shichi-Go-San preparations at Shizuoka Sengen Shrine, a ~105-meter climb up Mount Shizuhata complete with a Mount Fuji panorama, peaceful gardens in Sunpu Castle Park—including an accidental "album cover" on a park bench—and a glowing evening stroll down Aoba Symbol Road to Tokiwa Park.

## Fueling Up in Asakusa and a Not-Quite-Smooth Shinkansen Ride

The morning still started peacefully enough in Tokyo: over Japanese breakfast at *Onyado Nono Asakusa*, I laid down a proper foundation with rice, sashimi, chawanmushi, and braised beef before hauling the luggage back onto the rails.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-ShizuokaTag1/PXL_20261112_224009863.jpg" alt="Japanese breakfast tray at Onyado Nono Asakusa with sashimi over rice, chawanmushi, and beef" width="1200" height="675" >}}

Smooth as the farewell from Asakusa was, the onward leg on the Tōkaidō Shinkansen line turned out unusually bumpy. Defying its reputation for Swiss-watch precision, our Shinkansen had racked up a sizable delay today. Predictably, what happens on delayed trains when you rely on muscle memory for travel times happened right on cue: when the train stopped at **Shin-Fuji**, I stepped right off.  
Thankfully I glanced straight at my phone's GPS—and realized just in time that this wasn't Shizuoka yet! A quick sprint back onto the train salvaged the trip, so while the arrival definitely went down in the books as "less than smooth," the luggage made it safely to the hotel in Shizuoka shortly afterward.

## Shizuoka Hobby Square: Scale-Model Capital and a Tachikoma Encounter

The first deliberate stop right by the station was **Shizuoka Hobby Square** (静岡ホビースクエア). Shizuoka isn't called Japan's scale-modeling capital for nothing—heavyweights like Tamiya, Bandai, Hasegawa, and Aoshima all have their roots or factories in this region. As a *Ghost in the Shell* fan, peeking into the display cases immediately yielded a personal highlight: perched among various Gundam kits sat a meticulously painted, bright-blue **Tachikoma** from Section 9.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-ShizuokaTag1/PXL_20261113_041455557.jpg" alt="Blue model of a Tachikoma from Ghost in the Shell beside Gundam kits in a display case at Shizuoka Hobby Square" width="600" height="1067" >}}

The exhibits spanned everything from futuristic mechs to civil aviation—including an impressive **Airbus A380** model in ANA livery—all the way to unexpectedly playful niches.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-ShizuokaTag1/PXL_20261113_041905866.jpg" alt="Detailed display model of an Airbus A380 in ANA livery at Shizuoka Hobby Square" width="1200" height="675" >}}

Tamiya had a particularly quirky corner with its *Sweets Decoration* series: instead of tanks or race cars, entire gingerbread houses, cookie Christmas trees, icing figurines, and biscuits stamped with Tamiya's famous twin-star logo were recreated down to the tiniest detail out of plastic and decorative pastes. A brilliant palate cleanser after the chaotic arrival.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-ShizuokaTag1/PXL_20261113_042203193.jpg" alt="Miniature cookie landscape with Tamiya star biscuits and a gingerbread house from the Tamiya Sweets Decoration series" width="1200" height="675" >}}

## Shizuoka Sengen Shrine: Getting Ready for Shichi-Go-San

From the station, I walked northwest toward the historic **Shizuoka Sengen Shrine** (静岡浅間神社), which is actually an entire complex of several shrines nestled at the foot of Mount Shizuhata.  
Around **Yachihoko Shrine** (八千戈神社), with its intricate wood carvings and lacquered details, preparations for **Shichi-Go-San** (七五三)—the traditional November festival for children aged three, five, and seven—were still being set up: colorful banners were already in place, while overall the grounds were still pleasantly quiet.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-ShizuokaTag1/PXL_20261113_051843262.jpg" alt="The ornately decorated Yachihoko Shrine inside the Shizuoka Sengen Shrine complex with a Shichi-Go-San banner" width="1200" height="675" >}}

In front of the imposing two-story **Rōmon Gate** (*當國總社 富士新宮*), the first street stalls for chocolate bananas (*banana choco*) and *rakugaki senbei* were already lined up, while only a trickle of visitors strolled through the gate in the warm afternoon light.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-ShizuokaTag1/PXL_20261113_052351790.LONG_EXPOSURE-01.jpg" alt="Long exposure in front of the two-story Rōmon Gate of Shizuoka Sengen Shrine with festival stalls and blurred passersby" width="1200" height="675" >}}

## Up Mount Shizuhata: 105 Meters of Climb, Fuji Views, and a Jorō Spider

Behind the main buildings, a trail beckoned up the wooded slope of **Mount Shizuhata** (賤機山, *Shizuhatayama*). Halfway up, tucked amid tall trees, sits **Hayama Shrine** (麓山神社), its vermilion woodwork contrasting beautifully against the lush green and late-afternoon sun.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-ShizuokaTag1/PXL_20261113_054343857.jpg" alt="Hayama Shrine nestled among trees on the climb up Mount Shizuhata in warm afternoon light" width="1200" height="675" >}}

From the shrine forecourt at around 65 meters elevation, the path climbed onward to about **170 meters**—roughly **105 meters** of elevation gain, which made for a very pleasant hike in crisp 16 °C autumn air. At the top, a wide panoramic view over Shizuoka's sea of rooftops opened up between the branches.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-ShizuokaTag1/PXL_20261113_055546139.jpg" alt="Wide panoramic view from Mount Shizuhata over the city of Shizuoka to the surrounding hills" width="1200" height="675" >}}

And in the clear afternoon air, Japan's highest peak revealed itself in full glory too: rising majestically behind the green ridges to the northeast stood the snow-dusted cone of **Mount Fuji**—almost a little peace offering on the horizon after the involuntary stop at Shin-Fuji that morning.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-ShizuokaTag1/PXL_20261113_055806036.jpg" alt="Zoomed view from Mount Shizuhata toward the snow-capped summit of Mount Fuji behind the hills" width="1200" height="675" >}}

Heading toward *Kyūsei Kannon*, alongside an elderly analog thermometer, I ran into an impressive specimen of local wildlife: a huge black-and-yellow **Jorō spider** (*Trichonephila clavata*) had spun its sprawling web right beside the trail and was sunbathing in the backlight.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-ShizuokaTag1/PXL_20261113_061013550.jpg" alt="Close-up of a large black-and-yellow Jorō spider in its web along the forest edge of Mount Shizuhata" width="600" height="1067" >}}

For the descent, steep, arrow-straight stone stairs led back down beneath a dense canopy of leaves into the quiet shrine park—a wonderfully symmetrical view into the depths.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-ShizuokaTag1/PXL_20261113_062153399.jpg" alt="Looking down the steep stone staircase framed by a green canopy from Mount Shizuhata" width="600" height="1067" >}}

## Sunpu Castle Park & Momijiyama Garden: Still Water—and an Accidental Album Cover

Just a few minutes south of the shrine lies the expansive **Sunpu Castle Park** (駿府城公園), once the residence of Tokugawa Ieyasu. Tucked inside the park is the lovingly landscaped Japanese stroll garden **Momijiyama Garden** (紅葉山庭園). In the late afternoon light, pine trees, neatly pruned shrubs, and small rock formations reflected almost motionless in the dark pond.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-ShizuokaTag1/PXL_20261113_065153270.LONG_EXPOSURE-01.jpg" alt="Long exposure of the mirror-still pond in Momijiyama Garden at Sunpu Castle Park in golden afternoon light" width="1200" height="675" >}}

Immediately afterward in the park came what was probably the weirdest photo of the entire day, completely by accident. During a short breather, my smartphone was lying on a park bench with the camera still open and fired straight through the gap between two bench slats onto the asphalt below. Reddit and social media have that great *"Could be an album cover"* trend—and cropped to a fitting 1:1 square, this mishap actually pulls it off surprisingly well: two blurry diagonals in the foreground framing a bright, grainy ribbon of park path in between. Sometimes dumb luck delivers the cleanest minimalism.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-ShizuokaTag1/PXL_20261113_070216810_1x1.jpg" alt="Accidental 1:1 square shot between two park bench slats onto the bright stone path in album-cover style" width="800" height="800" >}}

## A Sea of Lights from the 21st Floor and an Evening Walk Down Aoba Symbol Road to Tokiwa Park

Once darkness fell, a free change of perspective was on the agenda: right on the edge of Sunpu Castle Park, the **Shizuoka Prefectural Government Office Annex** (静岡県庁別館) offers a publicly accessible observation lobby on the **21st floor** (about 144 meters up according to GPS).

From up there, Shizuoka at night stretched out as a glittering sea of lights with views over illuminated commercial streets, nearby shopping centers, and the evening skyline.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-ShizuokaTag1/PXL_20261113_082716802.NIGHT.jpg" alt="Night view from the 21st floor of the Shizuoka Prefectural Government Office over the lit-up city center" width="1200" height="675" >}}

The calm finale to this long day was an evening stroll through downtown along **Aoba Symbol Road** (青葉シンボルロード). This wide, tree-lined promenade flanked by little waterways cuts right through the city center and culminates at **Tokiwa Park** (常磐公園). In the dark, illuminated water fountains danced in front of the semicircular colonnade pavilion—while to the right above the trees, even a slender crescent moon hung in the night sky. After the morning's Shinkansen scare, a thoroughly redeeming end to our day in Shizuoka.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-ShizuokaTag1/PXL_20261113_090359438.jpg" alt="Illuminated fountain with tall water jets at night in Tokiwa Park at the end of Aoba Symbol Road" width="1200" height="675" >}}
