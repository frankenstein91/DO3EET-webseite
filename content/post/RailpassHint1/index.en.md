Hello from Japan!

I'm still here in Japan on vacation, but I wanted to share a quick tip with you about the Japan Rail Pass.

First, I should probably answer the basic question: what even is the Rail Pass, and what can you do with it?
The Rail Pass is comparable to Germany's BahnCard 100 and is available—from Japan's perspective—to foreign nationals. With this little card, you can ride JR trains (and use a few other services). Up until the end of 2023, it was an unbeatable bargain. Prices have since been raised, so you have to do the math a bit more carefully now.

And now for the tip…
If you're using the Rail Pass in Japan for spontaneous seat reservations, you basically have two options. There is also a third option—online booking—which I haven't used yet. Either way, my tip is specifically for using the ticket machines. When booking a seat reservation at a machine, you need your passport number and the Rail Pass QR code. So the workflow is: walk up to the machine, tap Rail Pass, scan the QR code, and then type in your passport number. That gets you into the menu for picking your train and seat. If, like me, you can't memorize your passport number and don't want to keep pulling out your passport every single time, this tip is for you!

First, download the Android app PassAndroid ([Google Play Store](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=org.ligi.passandroid&pcampaignid=web_share) or [F-Droid](https://f-droid.org/de/packages/org.ligi.passandroid/)). Once installed, create a new pass in the app. You can choose whatever title you like, though for fellow travelers I recommend something like `railpass` plus the person's name. Next, add your passport number as a front field. Then add the Rail Pass QR code to the pass and hit save. You can do this for your entire travel group—that way, whoever is operating the ticket machine has everyone's details ready in one place.
