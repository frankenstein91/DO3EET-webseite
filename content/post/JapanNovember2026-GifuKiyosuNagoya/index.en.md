The original plan for today looked completely different: start the morning in Kakegawa with a visit to **Kotonomama Hachimangū** (around 3–4 hours including transit), continue on to the **Site of Shichiri Ferry** and the **Nagoya Port Building**, and wrap up with dinner at **LaLaport Nagoya Minato AQULS**. A rainy Sunday, however, thoroughly upended that itinerary.

So we pivoted: instead of long outdoor stretches, we headed via Nagoya to **Gifu** to drop off our bags at the hotel, detoured to **Kiyosu** for a rainy-day visit to Hiyoshi Shrine and Kiyosu Castle, squeezed in an evening stroll along **Nagoya Port**, and finally rode out to **Chūbu Centrair International Airport** to pick up my mother's cousin. Along the way, we stumbled across an adorable restroom sign, a penguin mailbox, a seriously delicious new vending-machine hot chocolate, and an eerily deserted wing of the airport.

<!--more-->

## Weather Pivot and Arriving in Gifu

Leaving Kakegawa behind and passing through Nagoya, we arrived in **Gifu** in the late morning. Right outside the station, the city's best-known landmark gleams in bright gold: the statue of **Oda Nobunaga** draped in a vivid red cloak.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKiyosuNagoya/PXL_20261115_025215629.jpg" alt="Golden statue of Oda Nobunaga wearing a red cloak outside Gifu Station" width="600" height="1067" >}}

First, we dropped our suitcases at **Dormy Inn Gifu Ekimae** so we could travel light for the rest of the day. Next came a short break for a white hot chocolate at Starbucks inside **ASTY Gifu** (*アスティ岐阜*) right at the station *(not an ad—paid for myself)*—and right there on the restroom door hung a wonderfully cute laminated sign: alongside a little illustrated character saying *"今日も1日がんばろう！"* ("Let's do our best today too!"), the sign politely reads *"いつもきれいに使っていただきありがとうございます"* ("Thank you for always keeping it clean").

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKiyosuNagoya/PXL_20261115_041525610.jpg" alt="Cute Japanese restroom notice at ASTY Gifu thanking visitors for keeping the facilities clean" width="1200" height="675" >}}

## Rainy-Day Detour to Kiyosu: Hiyoshi Shrine and Kiyosu Castle

With the rain showing no signs of stopping, we swapped out our longer open-air plans for a closer alternative and rode via Owari-Ichinomiya (switching to the Meitetsu Line to Shin-Kiyosu) to **Kiyosu**.

### Kiyosu Sannōgū Hiyoshi Shrine (清洲山王宮 日吉神社)

Our first stop was **Kiyosu Sannōgū Hiyoshi Shrine**. Even just crossing the arched stone bridge (*taiko-bashi*) and passing through the dark wooden gate beneath rain-soaked trees set a wonderfully calm mood.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKiyosuNagoya/PXL_20261115_051807300.jpg" alt="Arched stone bridge and wooden gate at Kiyosu Sannōgū Hiyoshi Shrine in the rain" width="1200" height="675" >}}

On the shrine grounds sits the **Koumi-ishi (子産石)**—a dark, rain-slicked boulder revered as a childbirth and fertility stone, traditionally associated with Toyotomi Hideyoshi's mother.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKiyosuNagoya/PXL_20261115_051911640.jpg" alt="Sacred Koumi-ishi stone with an explanatory wooden sign at Kiyosu Hiyoshi Shrine" width="600" height="1067" >}}

Just a few steps further, a row of vermilion *torii* gates glowed vividly against the gray day, their red reflections stretching across the wet ground.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKiyosuNagoya/PXL_20261115_052041505.jpg" alt="Tunnel of bright red torii gates reflecting on the rain-wet path at Hiyoshi Shrine" width="600" height="1067" >}}

One of Hiyoshi Shrine's signature quirks is its choice of guardians: instead of the usual *komainu* or foxes, **monkeys (*Masaru* / 神猿)** wearing pointed hats, yellow robes, and holding *gohei* wands keep watch here. Nearby, a finely sculpted bronze dragon presides over the purification basin (*chōzuya*).

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKiyosuNagoya/PXL_20261115_052142456.jpg" alt="Guardian monkey statue Masaru wearing a yellow robe and holding a gohei wand on a stone pedestal" width="600" height="1067" >}}

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKiyosuNagoya/PXL_20261115_052317378.jpg" alt="Bronze dragon spout at the water purification basin of Kiyosu Hiyoshi Shrine" width="1200" height="675" >}}

After a brief pause in front of the main hall—where bright red accents along the dark roof gables stand out sharply against the rainy gray—flanked by its twin stone lanterns, we walked over to the nearby castle.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKiyosuNagoya/PXL_20261115_052519221.jpg" alt="Main hall of Kiyosu Sannōgū Hiyoshi Shrine with red roof accents framed by large stone lanterns in the rain" width="1200" height="675" >}}

### Kiyosu Castle (清洲城)

Stepping inside **Kiyosu Castle**—once a key stronghold of Oda Nobunaga—offered welcome shelter from the rain. Inside, an engaging exhibition covered Oda Nobunaga and samurai history before leading up to the rain-swept observation deck at the top of the keep, where I caught a close-up of a dark **shachihoko** roof ornament standing guard over the misty town.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKiyosuNagoya/PXL_20261115_062449156.jpg" alt="Close-up of a dark shachihoko roof ornament atop Kiyosu Castle" width="600" height="1067" >}}

Looking down reveals the manicured dry rock garden complete with bold *KIYOSU* lettering and the red bridge spanning the Gojō River. Right on cue, a white **Shinkansen** glided past on the elevated tracks through the rain.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKiyosuNagoya/PXL_20261115_062736193.jpg" alt="View from Kiyosu Castle looking down on the rock garden, castle gate, and red bridge" width="600" height="1067" >}}

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKiyosuNagoya/PXL_20261115_062957136.jpg" alt="A Shinkansen bullet train passing by near Kiyosu Castle on a misty, rainy afternoon" width="1200" height="675" >}}

Back outside in the garden, the black-and-white keep posed cleanly behind the illuminated white *KIYOSU* letters. Better yet, a local festival seemed to be underway right around the castle: despite the drizzle, a lively lineup of food trucks kept the atmosphere buzzing, and I grabbed a cheese dog along with a wonderfully crispy **40 cm churro** that was almost too long to hold in one hand.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKiyosuNagoya/PXL_20261115_071551298.jpg" alt="Exterior view of Kiyosu Castle behind the white KIYOSU sign in the rock garden" width="600" height="1067" >}}

## Brief Evening Stop at Nagoya Port: Icebreaker Fuji, Penguin Mailbox & Vending-Machine Cocoa

On our way toward the airport, we made a short stop at **Nagoyako (Nagoya Port)** so we could still check off at least one item from our original morning plan. Along the waterfront, the illuminated arch bridge, the distinctive **Nagoya Port Building**, and the orange Antarctic icebreaker **Fuji** (*5001*) glowed against the night sky.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKiyosuNagoya/PXL_20261115_083709427.jpg" alt="Illuminated Antarctic icebreaker Fuji beside the arch bridge and Nagoya Port Building at night" width="1200" height="675" >}}

I only walked past the outside of the **Port of Nagoya Public Aquarium** this time, but right out front (near *JETTY*) stood something I simply couldn't pass by without a photo: an adorable **penguin mailbox** (*郵便 POST*) perched on a faux snowdrift.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKiyosuNagoya/PXL_20261115_084730270.jpg" alt="Cute penguin-shaped mailbox marked POST outside the Port of Nagoya Public Aquarium" width="600" height="1067" >}}

A few steps further along the promenade, the blue-and-white **`@NAGOYA`** letters lit up the waterfront.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKiyosuNagoya/PXL_20261115_085012939.jpg" alt="Illuminated @NAGOYA sign along the Nagoya waterfront with the icebreaker and bridge behind" width="1200" height="675" >}}

And right there at the port at 17:51, a Japanese vending machine delivered a genuine highlight of the evening: **Sangaria "Choco & Milk"** (*とろけるおいしさ チョコ＆ミルク*). It was my first time spotting this variety, and it turned out to be seriously delicious—rich, creamy, full-bodied hot chocolate with none of the thin, watery taste some canned drinks suffer from. Just the thing to warm up in the damp evening air.

*(Note: Not an ad / not sponsored—I bought and paid for this drink myself at the vending machine.)*

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKiyosuNagoya/PXL_20261115_085153725.jpg" alt="Can of hot Sangaria Choco & Milk chocolate drink from a Japanese vending machine at Nagoya Port" width="600" height="1067" >}}

## Chūbu Centrair Airport: Eerie Silence in the "Center Pier Garden," Burgers & Pickup

Next, we rode the Meitetsu Airport Line to **Central Japan International Airport (Centrair)** to meet my mother's cousin off her flight. Inside the main terminal, a towering red Christmas tree complete with a glowing heart and world map already stood behind the *centrair* sign.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKiyosuNagoya/PXL_20261115_101830015.jpg" alt="Tall red Christmas tree with a glowing heart and world map at Chūbu Centrair International Airport" width="600" height="1067" >}}

While waiting for the arrival, I wandered off on my own to explore a bit and took an elevator down to the 1st floor toward the **"Center Pier Garden"** (*センターピアガーデン* / *Centrair Gallery*). Even inside the elevator, the panel stood out because button **3** was taped completely over in black.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKiyosuNagoya/PXL_20261115_102851771.jpg" alt="Elevator buttons at Centrair Airport showing Level 1 Center Pier Garden / Centrair Gallery and button 3 taped over" width="1200" height="675" >}}

When the doors opened, I was genuinely startled: the long, glass-walled **Center Pier Garden** hall lined with indoor trees lay almost completely deserted at 19:28—gleaming floors, bright ceiling trusses, and an uncanny dead silence. For an international airport, seeing a corridor that vast and empty felt downright eerie.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKiyosuNagoya/PXL_20261115_102835260.jpg" alt="Eerily empty Center Pier Garden corridor lined with indoor trees at Chūbu Centrair Airport" width="600" height="1067" >}}

Heading back to the livelier part of the terminal, I grabbed a fresh, juicy **Japanese Black Wagyu burger (*国産黒毛和牛バーガー*)** while still waiting for my mother's cousin to land.

{{< imgwebp src="https://do3eet-media.dreamofjapan.de/posts/JapanNovember2026-GifuKiyosuNagoya/PXL_20261115_105213926.jpg" alt="Japanese Black Wagyu burger with lettuce, tomato, onions, and sesame-herb topping in paper wrapper at Centrair Airport" width="600" height="1067" >}}

Shortly afterward, we met my mother's cousin safe and sound at Arrivals before heading for the train.

## Right on the Wire at 22:59: Yonaki Soba Back in Gifu

The Meitetsu train carried us straight back from the airport to **Meitetsu Gifu**, where we pulled off a buzzer-beater back at **Dormy Inn Gifu Ekimae**: at **22:59**—literally one minute before closing—we still caught a steaming bowl of **Yonaki Soba** (Dormy Inn's complimentary late-night soy-broth ramen topped with *aonori* seaweed, *menma*, scallions, and half an egg). Finished with a dash of that mildly spicy, fragrant yuzu-scented seasoning I always sprinkle over my noodles (likely *Yuzu Shichimi* seven-spice blend), there couldn't have been a better way to wind down after such a long, rainy, yet surprisingly eventful day.
