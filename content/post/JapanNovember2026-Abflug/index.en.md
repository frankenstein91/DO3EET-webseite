A quick sign of life and status update straight from transit: I'm sitting at Frankfurt Airport, and the next Japan trip has officially begun!

Part one went as smoothly as ever. **LH159** took me on the short morning hop from Leipzig/Halle (LEJ) to Frankfurt (FRA). In my headphones, I had just the right airborne entertainment: the StarTalk episode [*Spooky Science with Mary Roach*](https://open.spotify.com/episode/0PaGnJYSU5C1ojccAA4tH2?si=IAAq1aEfRx-4QTmw5RyWgg) with Neil deGrasse Tyson.

Now it's time to stretch my legs a bit and kill some time before the real travel highlight kicks off: the long-haul flight to Tokyo Haneda (HND) aboard **NH204**—operated by All Nippon Airways (ANA).

<!--more-->

---

## The Curse of Codeshare Bookings

The excitement for flying in the Dreamliner and returning to Japan is huge—yet one sad little fly in the ointment shadowed my trip prep: the notorious airline-alliance codeshare dilemma.

Because the ticket was booked through Lufthansa, requesting a special meal for the ANA leg was simply impossible in the system. Neither Lufthansa's booking interface nor ANA's manage-booking portal would let me enter dietary preferences—the Star Alliance partners' APIs just refused to talk to each other on this one.

And here I had been looking forward to a little culinary experiment at 33,000 feet:
* Either the **Seafood Meal (SFML)**, which tends to be fantastic on Japanese carriers,
* or the **Hindu Non-Vegetarian Meal (HNML)**, whose aromatic spices and quality frequent flyers regularly sing praises about.

Instead, it's time to sit back and see what today's regular inflight menu has to offer.

---

## Peeking at the Menu: A Truce Above the Clouds?

While waiting at the gate, I pulled up the official ANA menu for our flight to see what's being served on the way to Tokyo—and the outlook is anything but bleak.

For lunch/dinner after takeoff, there are two choices:
* **Japanese Beef Curry** with steamed rice
* **Sautéed Salmon with Butter**

On the side come dainty *kobachi* dishes with *tamagoyaki* (Japanese omelet), edamame, and mung bean sprouts, plus ice cream for dessert.

The best plot twist, however, awaits at breakfast shortly before our approach into Tokyo: alongside the traditional **Sanshoku-Don (三色丼)**—minced chicken, scrambled egg, and salmon flakes over rice—the Western option is none other than **Seafood & Gnocchi in Cream Sauce**!

Maybe my wish for a seafood meal at 33,000 feet will come true through the regular menu after all—no locked SFML code required.

Boarding for NH204 won't be long now. Next stop: Haneda!
